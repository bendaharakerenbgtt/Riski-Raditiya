# High-Converting Portfolio Builder

Guides an AI agent in collecting, structuring, and presenting a developer or designer's portfolio content to maximize hiring and freelancing conversion rates. The agent gathers deep project details interactively before generating any content or code, then structures the result around the sections that make a portfolio convert.

## When to Use

- Building a personal portfolio website from scratch or overhauling an existing one.
- The user wants to present 3-4 flagship projects, past roles, or freelance work to clients or employers.
- The user says any variation of "make my portfolio stand out" or "build me a portfolio".

## Core Principles (Why Portfolios Fail)

- **Originality over clones.** Cool animations and 3D effects do not convert clients if the design looks identical to every other template. Focus on original design choices and problem-solving thinking.
- **The 5-second rule.** A client decides whether to stay or leave within 5 seconds. The top fold (hero) must immediately communicate: (1) who you are, (2) what exact services or role you specialize in, (3) a direct, frictionless CTA to contact or hire you.
- **Quality over quantity.** Limit the portfolio to **3-4** outstanding projects. Avoid displaying 15-20 average projects, unfinished experiments, or standard tutorial clones (Todo app, Weather app, basic Calculator).

## Content Structure

### A. Hero Section (Above the Fold)

- **Headline:** Bold, clear statement of specialization and perspective — no vague corporate filler.
- **Sub-headline:** Summary of value proposition, thinking approach, and craftsmanship.
- **Primary CTA:** Direct, obvious contact or explore button plus frictionless navigation.

### B. Curated Projects Section

- Display only 3-4 flagship projects.
- **Each project card links to a dedicated project detail page or modal**, containing:
  - High-level summary and visual architecture preview.
  - Problem statement and target context.
  - Deep-dive into technical architecture and design decisions (10-point framework).
  - Key achievements, reflections, and lessons learned.

### C. Experience & Process Section

- Detail real problem-solving methods and learning philosophy.
- Highlight specific contributions and real-world results rather than generic job responsibilities.
- Make every claim concrete and verifiable, avoiding hollow buzzwords.

### D. Contact & Conversion Section

- Frictionless contact options: direct email, LinkedIn, profile links.
- Explicit response timeframe expectation.

## The 1-Minute Non-Tech Friend Test

- **Verification rule:** Can a non-technical person review the hero section for 10 seconds and accurately understand what you do and how to reach you?
- If **no**, simplify the jargon, clarify the headline, and make the primary CTA more visible.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Template-look design | Blends in with every other generic portfolio | Distinct editorial typography, original layout |
| Vague headline | Visitor cannot tell what you do in 5 seconds | Clear, authentic statement |
| 15-20 average projects | Dilutes the flagship work | Curate strictly to 3-4 outstanding projects |
| Tutorial-clone projects | Signals inexperience, not skill | Deep problem-solving case studies |
| Features over outcomes | Reads like a resume, not proof | Lead with problem, decisions, and lessons learned |
| Hidden or weak CTA | No obvious way to contact | Direct, visible CTA above the fold |
