Background & Current Journey

The Path That Brought Me Here

I have always been compelled by a simple need: to understand how things actually work beneath their surface descriptions. Whether examining a piece of software, a cognitive bias, a typographic system, or a mental model, I find deep satisfaction in taking things apart, identifying the invisible relationships between the parts, and reassembling them into something clear and usable.

My current journey sits at the intersection of cognitive psychology, educational design, and thoughtful digital engineering. Rather than chasing every fleeting technological fad or building products designed solely for engagement metrics, I am interested in tools and environments that expand human clarity, protect working memory, and encourage sustained independent thought.

I do not claim to be an all-knowing authority or an industry guru. I am an active student of the disciplines I engage with. Every project cataloged here is a record of a specific question I wanted to answer, a tension I wanted to resolve, or a tool I needed in order to think more clearly.

Methodology

My Learning Philosophy

Over years of deliberate study, I have distilled my approach into three guiding practices:

1. Explain It in Plain Language (The Feynman Standard)

If I cannot explain an idea in simple, unadorned prose to someone unfamiliar with the field, I do not yet understand it. Jargon is frequently a hiding place for conceptual vagueness.

2. Build to Understand, Not Just to Ship

Reading about a concept creates the illusion of mastery; building an interactive simulation, an ergonomic parser, or a diagnostic tool forces you to confront every edge case and logical contradiction.

3. Practice Editorial Restraint

The easiest thing in design and code is to add more: more features, more animations, more dependencies, more words. The hardest and most rewarding discipline is knowing what to subtract to let the essential core breathe.

Aspirations

Current Direction
